Extreme macro visualization of dense collagen fibrils (<11 nm pores) sterically trapping tumor nucleosomes during dormancy until systemic inflammatory stress triggers matrix remodeling, releasing fluorescent ctDNA directly into capillary circulation.

  • ✦ Steric Pore Cutoff: Dense type-I collagen pores (≤11 nm) block 166 bp nucleosomes, explaining false-negative liquid biopsies (MRD⁻)

  • ✦ Trans-Endothelial Influx: Double-stranded helical ctDNA streams across the dilated matrix into capillary bloodstream

Part 3 · Why Chemo Can't Penetrate: The Desmoplastic Shield

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) tighten thick collagen cables, mechanically compressing capillary microvessels shut. High interstitial fluid pressure halts convection, stranding drug molecules while dormant persister cells survive untouched in the deep stromal sanctuary.

  • ✦ Biomechanical Compression: Contractile CAFs squeeze microvessels shut, arresting convective delivery
  • ✦ Stalled Drug Diffusion: Elevated interstitial fluid pressure creates a steep Bessel K₀ boundary outside the vessel wall
  • ✦ Silent Persister Sanctuaries: Dormant G₀/G₁ tumor persister cells survive completely shielded from cytotoxic therapy

Conventional CT scans cannot detect sub-radiological microscopic recurrence (~10⁸–10⁹ cells). Continuous reaction-diffusion shedding kinetics predict an analytical ctDNA lead time of 5.46 months: liquid biopsy flags molecular relapse (MRD⁺) months before macroscopic lesions appear on imaging.
